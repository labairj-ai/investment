# Separate Structural Macro Exposure from Current Regime Inputs

- **ID:** 0464
- **Status:** done
- **Created:** 2026-09-19
- **Priority:** high
- **Depends:** 0463

## Problem

The four holding macro scores (rate sensitivity, inflation hedge, USD sensitivity, geopolitical) are generated by feeding both the ticker and today's live macro conditions (VIX, yield, CPI, curve, dollar) into the same LLM prompt. This causes the model to shift a company's structural exposure score when the macro environment changes, even though the company itself hasn't changed. The result is noisy week-over-week deltas that reflect macro regime movement rather than genuine changes in company exposure — and a downstream "AI rationalization loop" where a second LLM explains why the environment justified a change that was actually random prompt variance.

## Proposed approach

- Rename the current four scores to "structural exposure" and strip current regime inputs from the scoring prompt. Structural exposure should only change when company fundamentals change (earnings, business model shift, M&A, etc.).
- Build a separate `MacroRegime` object from raw observations: rate stress (yield level + direction + curve shape), inflation stress (CPI trend + breakevens), dollar stress (DXY/UUP direction + magnitude), volatility stress (VIX level + trend), geopolitical stress (user-maintained or rules-based).
- Combine the two in a final step: `current_risk = exposure × regime_stress` per dimension.
- Update scoring prompts so they only ask for structural exposure; regime is computed deterministically, not LLM-invented.
- Decide: how often should structural exposure re-score? Proposal: weekly for LLM pass, but only write a new row when a material change is detected or a quarterly refresh is due.

Options & Covered Calls — Core Mechanics

Covered Call (Buy-Write)
Selling a call option against shares you already own. You collect the premium immediately; in exchange you agree to sell your shares at the strike price if the buyer exercises. The strategy trades upside potential for income.
Strike Price (K)
The price at which your shares would be called away (sold) if the option is exercised. You choose a strike above the current price to stay out-of-the-money.
DTE — Days to Expiration
Calendar days until the option contract expires. The dashboard filters 21–60 DTE by default (enough time value without excessive event risk).
Bid / Ask / Mid
Bid — highest price a buyer will pay right now. Ask — lowest price a seller will accept. Mid — the mathematical midpoint. Wide bid-ask spreads indicate an illiquid market; the mid is rarely the actual fill price.
Exec Premium
Exec = Bid + 0.25 × (Ask − Bid)
Estimated fill price — more realistic than mid for thinly traded options. Uses 25% of the spread above bid, reflecting typical retail execution. All profit, yield, and alpha calculations use Exec Premium, not Mid.
Premium % (Prem%)
Prem% = Exec Premium / Stock Price
The option income as a percentage of the stock's current value. Useful for comparing contracts across different price stocks.
Annualized Return (Ann%)
Ann% = Prem% × (365 / DTE)
Premium yield scaled to a full year. Used for comparison only — it assumes you can continuously roll at the same premium, which is unrealistic. The V2 engine ranks by Score, not Ann%, to avoid systematically favouring short-DTE contracts.
P/L if Called
(Strike + Exec Premium − Avg Cost) / Avg Cost
Your total return on the original investment if assigned. This is the number that matters for profit-floor filtering; premium counts toward the return. Floor policy: the engine uses your position-level average cost (not individual tax-lot basis). In a FIFO scenario where you hold lots at varying prices, a high-cost lot could be assigned at a loss even when the average-cost floor is satisfied — the dashboard does not model this per-lot edge case.
Assignment
The option buyer exercises their right to buy your shares at the strike price. For covered calls this typically happens at expiration when the stock closes above the strike, though early assignment is possible for deep ITM contracts near ex-dividend dates.
ITM / ATM / OTM
In The Money (ITM) — strike < current price; intrinsic value exists, assignment more likely. At The Money (ATM) — strike ≈ current price; maximum time value. Out of The Money (OTM) — strike > current price; all value is extrinsic; lower assignment risk, lower premium.
Intrinsic Value
max(Stock Price − Strike, 0)
The in-the-money amount of the option. An OTM call has zero intrinsic value — its entire price is time/extrinsic value.
Extrinsic Value (Time Value)
Option Price − Intrinsic Value
The portion of the option's price beyond intrinsic value. Decays toward zero at expiration. Relevant for early assignment risk: a holder generally won't exercise early if doing so forfeits meaningful extrinsic value.
Open Interest (OI)
Total number of outstanding contracts at this strike/expiry. Higher OI means tighter spreads and easier fills. The dashboard's liquidity score weights OI at 30 points out of 100.
Roll / Roll Up / Roll Out
Roll — buy back the current call and sell a new one simultaneously. Roll out — same strike, later expiry (collects more time value). Roll up — higher strike, same or later expiry (raises your cap, often a debit or smaller credit). A roll is evaluated as a new trade: it should only be done when the net position beats both holding and closing outright.

V2 Analysis Metrics

CC Alpha $
CC Alpha ≈ P − E[(ST − K)+]
where E[(ST − K)+] = S·e(μ−q)T·N(d1,μ) − K·N(d2,μ)
d1,μ = d2,μ + σ√T  ·  σ = eff_IV
The expected gain from selling the call versus simply continuing to hold the stock. The expected upside surrender is computed under the real-world lognormal model with drift μ — not the risk-neutral rate. The premium P is collected today; the surrender term occurs at expiration. The formula implicitly assumes r = 0 (i.e., no time-value adjustment to P). For typical 21–60 DTE contracts and a 4–5% risk-free rate the error is <$0.03 on a $3 premium — immaterial in practice.

Positive (green) — selling the call adds expected value vs holding.
Negative (red) — holding outright has higher expected return; consider doing nothing.
Regret %
Regret % = N(d2,μ(B))   where B = K + P
d2,μ(B) = [ln(S/B) + (μ − q − 0.5σ²)T] / (σ√T)
Probability that the stock closes above the regret threshold B = K + P at expiration — the price above which selling the call leaves you worse off than simply holding. Uses the same physical distribution as Estimated Expiry ITM% and CC Alpha (identical μ, q, σ = eff_IV); regret_prob = itm_prob_real(S, K+P, T, σ, μ, q). This is lower than the expiry-ITM probability: the stock can finish above the strike and you still win, as long as it doesn't run past K + P.
Regret Threshold
Regret Threshold = Strike + Exec Premium
The stock price above which the covered call underperforms a pure hold. Below this level, even if assigned, you captured more value than you gave up.
Score (Multi-Factor)
Score = 100 × (0.25·A + 0.15·Y + 0.15·V + 0.15·L + 0.15·U + 0.15·R)
0–100 composite for within-ticker contract selection. Uses percentile ranks computed across all contracts for this ticker and expiration set, so a 30-DTE 87 and a 55-DTE 87 for the same stock are genuinely comparable. All inputs ∈ [0,1] before weighting:
A — CC Alpha (25%) — percentile rank; higher alpha = better
Y — Premium yield (15%) — percentile rank
V — IV richness (15%) — percentile rank (clipped at −1 before ranking)
L — Liquidity score (15%) — absolute normalised (Liq/100), not a percentile
U — Upside room (15%) — percentile rank of vol-normalised distance to strike
R — 1 − PctRank(Regret%) (15%) — inverse percentile rank so lower regret = higher score

Limitation: because ranks are within-ticker, every stock will produce a contract near 90. Do not compare Score across different tickers. Use Opp Score for cross-ticker comparison.
Opp Score (Opportunity Score)
OppScore = 100 × (0.30·A + 0.20·Y + 0.20·V + 0.15·L + 0.15·R)
using fixed reference scales, not percentile ranks
Cross-ticker comparable score — MSFT 72 and RIVN 72 represent similar opportunity levels. Uses absolute inputs with fixed reference scales so the score doesn't inflate just because a contract ranks well within a bad option chain:
A — CC Alpha / S; reference 0–2.5% per contract
Y — Annualized yield; reference 0–20%/yr
V — IV richness; reference −50% to +100%
L — Liquidity score / 100 (absolute)
R — 1 − (Regret% / 40%); 0–40% regret range

Pair with Score: Score answers "which contract is best for this stock?"; Opp Score answers "which stock currently offers the best covered-call opportunity?"
Liquidity Score
0–100 composite measuring how tradeable the contract is: 50 pts for bid-ask spread quality (<5% of mid = max points), 30 pts for open interest, 20 pts for daily volume. Affects the Score ranking. The Exec Premium formula (Bid + 0.25 × spread) is fixed — liquidity does not change the λ coefficient.

Probability & Greeks

Delta (Δ)
Δ = e−qT · N(d1)
Rate of change of the option's price per $1 move in the stock. Delta is displayed in the table (e.g., 0.26) as a Greeks measure. It is sometimes cited as a rough assignment-probability estimate ("25-delta call"), but this is an approximation for two reasons: (1) the true risk-neutral ITM probability is N(d2), which is somewhat lower than delta for low-dividend equities; (2) neither delta nor N(d2) accounts for real-world stock drift. The table shows delta separately from eITM% (the real-world model's ITM estimate) and Regret % for a cleaner probability picture.
Estimated Expiry ITM %
P(ST > K) = N(d2,μ)
d2,μ = [ln(S/K) + (μ − q − 0.5σ²)T] / (σ√T),  σ = eff_IV (contract IV if > 1%, else HV forecast)
Estimated probability that the stock price closes above the strike at expiration under the real-world drift model. This is not the same as assignment probability: American equity calls can be exercised early (particularly around dividends), and an ITM finish doesn't guarantee assignment in all scenarios. Use this as a directional estimate of how likely the strike is breached at expiry. If μ < r, this will be lower than the risk-neutral N(d2); for a strong-momentum stock it will be higher.
μ — Real-World Drift
μ = 0.50·μ60d + 0.25·μ252d + 0.25·μmarket
Blended estimate of the stock's total-return drift, estimated from yfinance dividend-adjusted Close prices (auto_adjust=True). Weights recent 60-day momentum most heavily, blends in 1-year trend and a 10% long-run market assumption, then caps at ±50% to prevent extreme momentum from dominating. In d2,μ the dividend yield q is subtracted separately so the formula captures price drift only. Displayed as μ +X.X%/yr.
Gamma (Γ)
Γ = e−qT · φ(d1) / (S · σ · √T)
Rate of change of delta per $1 move in the stock. High gamma (near expiry and near the strike) means delta can shift dramatically on a small stock move. Used in the open position evaluator to flag accelerating assignment risk: a 1% stock move changes delta by approximately Γ × 0.01 × S.

Volatility & Pricing

IV — Implied Volatility
The market's expectation of future volatility, backed out from current option prices using Black-Scholes. Higher IV = more expensive options = more premium income for sellers. Shown as ATM IV (at-the-money, nearest expiry).
HV — Historical (Realised) Volatility
Actual volatility the stock has exhibited, measured from daily price returns and annualised. The dashboard computes HV20 (20-day), HV60 (60-day), HV120 (120-day), and an EWMA variant (exponentially weighted, λ=0.94, weights recent moves more heavily).
HV_forecast
HV_fc = 0.40·HV20 + 0.35·HVEWMA + 0.25·HV60
Blended forward-looking volatility estimate used as the denominator in IV Richness. Weights recent realised vol more heavily than longer windows.
HV Percentile
Percentile of today's 21-day realised volatility within its own 1-year distribution. HV Pct 70% means today's vol is higher than 70% of the past year's readings — a better environment for selling premium. Note: this is a percentile (fraction of observations below current), not a conventional rank (HVcurr − HVmin) / (HVmax − HVmin), which can differ substantially when the historical distribution has outliers.
IV Richness
IV RichnessK,T = IVK,T / HV_forecast − 1
Whether the option is expensive or cheap relative to the model's expected realised volatility. Each contract uses its own implied volatility (not the ATM IV from the nearest expiry shown in the header), so a contract can appear rich due to strike-specific skew rather than a broadly elevated vol environment.
Positive (green, "rich") — market IV exceeds the model's expected realized vol, which is generally more favorable for premium sellers, all else equal. Part of any premium can reflect compensation for jump risk, gap risk, or other tail risks not captured in historical vol.
Negative (red, "cheap") — options are priced below expected realized vol; a less favorable environment for selling.
Distinct from HV Percentile, which measures realized vol vs. its own history.
Expected Move
Expected Move = S × IV × √T
Approximate one-standard-deviation move in the stock over the option's life, in dollar terms (S × IV × √T). This is the standard trader approximation; strictly, volatility applies to log-returns, so the formula slightly understates the true sigma move. A strike 1.0× the expected move above spot is roughly 1-sigma OTM. Used to normalise strike distance across stocks with very different volatilities.

Portfolio & Tax

Layers (L1–L5)
Portfolio architecture classification: L1 Structural Ballast (index funds, BRK.B) · L2 Cash-Flow Engines (dividend payers ≥3% yield) · L3 Compounders (quality growth) · L4 Convexity / Optionality (high upside, high risk — Taleb barbell 10–15%) · L5 Shock Absorbers (low-correlation hedges).
TWR — Time-Weighted Return
A return calculation that eliminates the distorting effect of cash flows (new money added, positions sold). Each day's return is computed independently and then chain-multiplied. This makes the portfolio chart comparable to SPY regardless of when capital was deployed.
FIFO — First In, First Out
When selling shares, the oldest lot (lowest purchase date) is consumed first. IRS default cost basis method. Affects whether a sale is short-term or long-term and which cost basis applies to the gain/loss calculation.
Tax Lot
A specific purchase tranche: a date, share count, and cost/share. Each lot ages independently toward long-term status (held more than one year, per IRS rules). The Lots modal shows all lots per holding with their ST/LT badge, unrealized G/L, and days until LT conversion.
ST / LT — Short-Term / Long-Term Gains
Short-term — held ≤1 year; taxed as ordinary income (up to 37% federal). Long-term — held more than 1 year (IRS rule: the holding period begins the day after acquisition; sale must occur on a date strictly later than the one-year anniversary); taxed at lower capital gains rates (0%, 15%, or 20% depending on income).

Written equity call tax treatment depends on how the position closes (IRS Pub. 550): If the call expires or is closed (bought back), the option gain/loss is generally short-term capital gain/loss regardless of how long it was open. If the call is exercised, the premium is added to the proceeds from selling the underlying shares, and the resulting stock gain/loss generally follows the stock's holding period (short- or long-term). Note: qualified-covered-call and straddle rules (IRC §1092) can alter the holding-period treatment of the underlying shares. Consult a tax advisor for your specific situation.
NIIT — Net Investment Income Tax
3.8% federal surtax on investment income (dividends, capital gains, net option gains) for taxpayers above $200k (single) / $250k (MFJ) MAGI. NIIT applies to net option gains, not the gross premium collected — losses and offsetting positions reduce the NII base. Per IRS rules, NIIT applies to the lesser of: (a) total net investment income (NII) or (b) the excess of MAGI over the applicable threshold. Formula: 0.038 × min(NII, max(0, MAGI − Threshold)). The dashboard applies this lesser-of formula using the representative MAGI for your selected bracket. Toggle in the Tax Harvesting modal.
Yield on Cost
Yield on Cost = Annual Dividend per Share / Avg Cost per Share
Dividend yield relative to what you paid, not the current market price. A position bought cheaply years ago may show a much higher yield on cost than the current quoted yield.
Ex-Dividend Date
The date you must own shares before to receive the next dividend. Buy on or after this date and you miss the payout. For covered calls, an ex-div date inside the option window creates early assignment risk: a call holder may exercise early to capture the dividend if the dividend exceeds the option's extrinsic value.

Buffett Screener

Gross Margin
(Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold) / Revenue
How much of each revenue dollar remains after direct production costs. Buffett threshold: ≥40%. High gross margins indicate pricing power and durable competitive advantage.
SG&A / Gross Profit
Selling, General & Administrative expenses as a fraction of gross profit. Buffett threshold: ≤30%. Companies spending less to maintain revenue tend to have stronger moats.
Interest / Operating Income
Debt service burden relative to operating earnings. Buffett threshold: ≤15%. Low ratio = company can service debt comfortably even in a downturn.
CapEx / Net Income
Capital expenditure intensity. Buffett threshold: ≤25% (screener uses ≤50% for a wider net). Capital-light businesses generate free cash flow without heavy reinvestment, enabling buybacks and dividends.
EV / EBITDA
Enterprise Value divided by Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation. Capital-structure neutral valuation multiple. Used in the Buffett Deep-Dive and Recommended Purchases valuation scoring.
P / FCF
Price divided by Free Cash Flow per share. FCF = operating cash flow minus capital expenditures — the actual cash the business generates after maintaining its asset base. Often more reliable than P/E for capital-intensive industries.
